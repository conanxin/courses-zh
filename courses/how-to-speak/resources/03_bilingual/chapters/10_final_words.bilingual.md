## Segment 1: Final Words Overview / 第一段：结束语概览

**English:**

You got your final slide up there. It's a contribution slide. Somehow you have to tell people you're finished. So let's see it, check out a few possibilities.

One thing you could do in the final words is you could tell a joke.

It's OK. By the time you're done, people have adjusted themselves to your voice parameters. They're ready for a joke.

I was sitting in another bar, this time in Austin, Texas with a colleague of mine named Doug Lenat. And Doug's a fantastic speaker.

And so I said to Doug, Doug, you're a fantastic speaker, what's your secret?

And he said, oh, I always finish with a joke, and that way, people think they've had fun the whole time.
[LAUGHTER]

So yeah, a joke will work down there.

**中文译文:**

你已经把最后一张幻灯片挂上去了，那是一张**贡献**幻灯片。

但你还是得告诉听众——**你已经讲完了**。

所以让我们来看一看，探讨几种可能性。

**结束语里可以做的第一件事**，是讲一个笑话。

可以啊。等你讲完的时候，听众已经适应了你的声音参数，他们已经准备好听一个笑话了。

我曾坐在另一家酒吧——这次是在得克萨斯州的奥斯汀，跟我的一位同事道格·莱纳特（Doug Lenat）一起。道格是个非常出色的演讲者。

于是我对道格说："道格，你是一位非常出色的演讲者，你的秘诀是什么？"

他说："噢，我每次都用一个笑话收尾，这样大家就会觉得从头到尾都很开心。"
[笑声]

是的，笑话在结尾是有效的。

## Segment 2: Why "Thank You" Is a Weak Ending / 第二段：为什么"谢谢"是一招弱棋

**English:**

How about this one?

Thank you.

I don't recommend it. It's a weak move. You will not go to hell if you conclude your talk by saying thank you, but it's a weak move, and here's why.

When you say thank you, even worse, thank you for listening, it suggests that everybody has stayed that long out of politeness and that they had a profound desire to be somewhere else. But they're so polite, they stuck it out. And that's what you're thanking them for.

So once wild applause has started, you can mouth a thank you, and there's nothing wrong with that. But the last thing you do should not be saying thank you.

Now, you say to me, well, doesn't everybody say thank you?

Well, what everybody does is not necessarily the right thing.

And I like to illustrate how some talks can end without saying thank you.

I like to draw from political speeches, but the ones that I've heard recently aren't so good, so—
[LAUGHTER]

So I'm going to have to go go back a little bit.

**中文译文:**

那这一种呢？

**谢谢。**

我不推荐。这是很弱的一招。你若是拿"谢谢"收尾，并不会下地狱——但确实是一招弱棋，原因如下。

当你说"谢谢"，更糟的是说"谢谢你们的聆听"——它**暗示了每个人能留到现在只是出于礼貌**——他们其实**打心底更想去别的地方**，只是因为太有礼貌，所以硬撑到了最后。

而这正是你在感谢他们的东西。所以一旦**热烈的掌声响起**，你可以**无声地做个"谢谢"的口型**，那样**没问题**。但你**最后做的那个动作**不应该就是说"谢谢"。

你会对我说："可是，难道不是每个人都说'谢谢'吗？"

嗯——**人人都在做的不一定就是对的**。

我喜欢举一些"不需要用谢谢也能结尾"的例子——我倾向于从政治演讲里取材，但我最近听过的一些并不太好——
[笑声]

所以我得往回翻一些年。

## Segment 5: Stock Endings & Salute / 第五段：惯例式结尾与致敬

**English:**

And it's interesting that over time, people figure this out, and there's some stock ways of ending things.

So in the Catholic church, and the good old Latin mass, it ended with ite missa est, which translates approximately to OK, the mass is over, you can go home now.
[LAUGHTER]

And of course, at musical concerts, you know that it's time to clap not at the end of the song, but rather when the conductor goes over and shakes hands with the concert master. Those are conventions that tell you that the event is over.

So those are all possibilities for here.

But one more possibility, and that is that you can salute the audience.

And by that, I mean, you can say something about how much you value your time at a place.

So I could say, well, it's been great fun being here. It's been fascinating to see what you folks are doing here at MIT. I've been much stimulated and provoked by the kinds of questions you've been asking, it's been really great. And I look forward to coming back on many occasions in the future.

So that salutes the audience. You can do that.

**中文译文:**

有趣的是，随着时间的推移，人们慢慢地把这件事琢磨透了，于是出现了一些"惯用结尾"。

比如在天主教会、在古拉丁弥撒里，弥撒是以"**ite missa est**"结束的。它大致的翻译是"好的，弥撒结束了，你们可以回家了。"

[笑声]

当然，在音乐会上——你知道该鼓掌的时刻**不是歌曲结束的刹那**，而是**指挥走过去跟乐队首席握手的那一刻**。这些就是告诉你"演出结束了"的惯例。

所以这些都是可选的结尾方案。

但还有**另一种可能性**，那就是**向观众敬礼（salute the audience）**。

我所谓"敬礼"，意思是：你可以**表达你对"在这里"的感激**。

比如我可以说："嗯，在这里真的非常开心。能看到你们这些 MIT 的人在做的事情，**令人着迷**。你们一路上提的这些问题让我**倍受激发、深受刺激**——真的非常棒。我期待今后**能以多种方式再回访**。"

这就是"向观众敬礼"。你可以这么做。

## Key Points / 本章要点

- **Three legitimate closing moves**: joke, salute the audience, stock/conventional ending.
  三类可用的结束方式：笑话、致敬观众、惯例式结尾。
- **Never let "thank you" be your last words**: it implies the audience only stayed for politeness' sake; the silent mouthing is fine *after* applause begins.
  不要让"谢谢"成为你的最后一句——掌声起来后做个无声的口型可以。
- **Stock endings borrow cultural signaling**: Catholic mass ("ite missa est"), concert handshake, political benediction.
  惯例式结尾借用文化信号：天主教弥撒、音乐会的握手礼、政治演讲的祝福式。
- **Bill Clinton's close**: deliberately purses his lips to avoid saying thank you, uses a small salute instead.
  克林顿的结尾：刻意抿住嘴唇以免说出"谢谢"，改用一个小型敬礼。
- **The audience, not the topic, deserves the closing tribute**: salute them for caring about packaging itself.
  值得被致敬的，是"愿意到场关心包装本身"的观众，而不是演讲的主题。
- **"What everyone does is not necessarily right."** Imitation of the most common move is rarely a virtue.
  "人人都在做的不一定就是对的"。模仿最常见的那一招，往往不是优点。

## Practicable Methods / 可实践方法

1. **Pre-commit to one closing move** before you take the stage: joke / salute / stock.
   演讲前先决定用哪一类结尾：笑话 / 致敬 / 惯例。
2. **Salute template**: 30 seconds naming 2–3 concrete things you value about the host / audience / place.
   "致敬观众"模板：30 秒内点出 2–3 件你感恩的具体事物。
3. **Steal a stock ending**: borrow one culturally familiar closing signal; practice its delivery.
   惯例式结尾实验：借一个熟悉的文化仪式作为结尾信号，练熟它。
4. **Replace "thank you" with silent mouthing after applause**.
   把"谢谢"换成掌声响起的瞬间做无声口型。
5. **Re-peruse old political speeches for endings**: tag each one as joke / salute / stock; build your closing-bank.
   从政治演讲里学结尾：把它们一一标注为笑话/致敬/惯例，建成你的素材库。
